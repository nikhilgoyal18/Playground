## Jay Stansell from Product Coalition
*1 issue this period*

### We Built Netflix-Style Recommendations Without a Single LLM Call `Product`
- Core decision: deleted LLM-based recommendation engine and built deterministic system instead
- Problem solved: LLM couldn't bridge Don Norman's "Gulf of Evaluation" — couldn't explain why it recommended specific content
- Five scoring factors grounded in learning science (each weighted, each defensible):
  1. **Content Relevance**: structured mapping between user goals, role, industry and content metadata
  2. **Difficulty Match**: based on Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development and Csikszentmihalyi's Flow theory; Mastery component from Daniel Pink's Drive framework
  3. **Skill Gaps**: Kano Model "must-be" quality — identifying where user is weakest relative to needs
  4. **Recency**: spaced repetition principle; Kahneman & Tversky's memory availability research
  5. **Diversity**: variable reward design from Hook Model — nudge users into unfamiliar territory to break comfortable loops
- Output: every recommendation traces back to defensible reasoning (e.g., "recommended because you haven't practiced this skill in 12 days, this is one level above your last difficulty, targets widest gap")
- Framework references: Norman (Gulf of Evaluation), Nielsen's Heuristics #1 (Visibility of System Status)

### AI Moves Fast. These Five Things Don't Change. `Product`
- First principle: as AI makes solutions faster, correctly diagnosing the real customer problem becomes MORE valuable, not less; "the diagnostic work, sitting with customers, watching where they hesitate... that is the real work"
- Persistent gap: the gap between what customers say they want and what they actually do; Nielsen's Heuristics #6 (Recognition over Recall); users ignore added features and default to simplest path
- Shipping velocity ≠ validation velocity: "you can automate the build. You cannot automate the learning"; human experience of the solution cannot be compressed
- Pricing and value fit: psychological and contextual problem, not a data modeling problem; value communication and packaging sit in someone's mind
- Product-market fit signal reading: retention curves, word-of-mouth pull, churn rate, Sean Ellis question response—these require judgment and context that AI lacks
- The loop: diagnose problem → watch real behavior → validate solution → check value exchange → read market fit → repeat; prioritization and deciding what to kill require skin in the game
