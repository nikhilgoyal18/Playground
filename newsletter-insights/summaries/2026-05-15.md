### Stop Telling Your Brain You Can't Focus `Other`
- Every time you say "I can't focus" or "I have ADHD," you are giving your brain an instruction — it is always working to confirm your sense of identity and keep you consistent with who you believe you are
- The key distinction: "I've been diagnosed with ADHD" (medical history) vs. "I am ADHD" (identity) — the latter is a self-fulfilling prophecy your brain actively enforces
- "I am" are the two most powerful words you'll use — they are literal instructions to your brain for who you are; your self-concept is the operating system your brain runs on
- The reframe: replace "I can't focus" with "I am building my ability to focus" or simply "I can focus" — say it especially when it doesn't feel true, because that's the moment the rewiring starts
- Focus is not a personality trait — it is a skill, and skills are built through deliberate practice and repetition

### Transitioning Off ADHD Medication Through Neuroplasticity `Other`
- Em holds a PhD in neuroscience and studied drug addiction/pharmacology — her lens on ADHD medication is scientific, not anecdotal
- What she learned: the "side effects" of stimulants (heart racing, skin breakouts, sleep disruption, irritability, being easily triggered) are not side effects — they are **off-target effects**, the drug doing exactly what it does to your nervous system, specifically chronic overactivation of the sympathetic nervous system (fight-or-flight branch)
- After transitioning off stimulants, she used neuroplasticity-based techniques to rewire focus capacity; the proof point: previously she would tear the house apart looking for lost keys; post-rewiring, she paused, got quiet, asked herself where they were, and the answer came — behavioral evidence of structural brain change
- The core claim: "I sat in a library and read or wrote for seven hours straight, completely focused, no medication, no stimulants" — she frames this as achievable, not exceptional, because focus is trainable
- Key principle: neuroplasticity means the brain's wiring is not fixed; the story you tell yourself about your brain is the ceiling on what your brain will attempt

- The counterintuitive finding: half of PM Twitter is focused on learning Claude Code, but Anthropic PMs get more leverage from the Cowork/"everything else" bucket; an agent wired into Calendar, Gmail, Slack, and Drive gives back more PM time than any amount of prompting inside Cursor
- **Scott White (Claude.ai PM):** "Evals are the new PRD" — his workflow: write the PRD, generate evals from it, implement those evals inside the product itself; Cat Wu adds that evals make "an abstract product feel more concrete"; converting a PRD into 5 sharp test cases is the fastest way to find out if your spec is precise enough to act on
- **Side quests > roadmaps:** Cat Wu's team runs self-directed experiments outside the formal plan — Claude Code on Desktop, the AskUserQuestion tool, and the entire todo-list system inside Claude Code were none of them on a roadmap; carve out one afternoon a week to build something nobody asked for

### A Guide to Event-Driven Architectural Patterns `Engineering`
- Synchronous direct-call communication works for small systems but produces three failure modes at scale: **tight coupling** between services, **fragile failure behavior** (one slow service blocks the chain), and **bottlenecks** at the slowest component in any call chain
- Event-driven architecture (EDA) is the alternative: services publish events when something meaningful happens; other services react on their own time — the fundamental shift from push-and-wait to publish-and-react
- EDA doesn't eliminate problems — it introduces its own, and the architectural patterns covered in the article are the established techniques for solving those specific new problems
- The article covers **6 EDA patterns** (body was truncated before the patterns were named, but this is the framing lens: each pattern exists to address a specific failure mode that synchronous architecture avoids but EDA introduces)
- Key design principle implied: choosing EDA is trading one category of problems (coupling, brittleness) for another (eventual consistency, event ordering, at-least-once delivery) — the patterns are tools for managing that tradeoff deliberately

### The Date Rewind — Moving Past Surface-Level Dating Conversation `Other`
- The **5 Levels of Communication** framework: most dates stall at Level 3 (trading facts) and never reach Level 5 (sharing how you actually feel about each other) — the gap is not awkwardness, it's the absence of a technique to bridge it
- The **Date Rewind technique**: pick a specific moment from the last date you're curious about and ask about their inner world directly — this creates low-stakes surface area to discuss actual feelings without jumping to "what are we?"
- Example openers: *"I keep thinking about when we were at that park and I said [X]. What was going on in your head?"* or *"When you texted me [X], I was so curious what was happening for you there."*
- Bold variant: *"I'm curious what you thought when you first saw my profile — are you down to debrief it together?"* then pull out your phone and scroll through together — this creates a shared physical experience (leaning over the same screen) which accelerates intimacy through co-presence
- The meta-insight: debriefing a date directly with your date is like gossiping about yourselves — dissecting a shared experience only you two had generates intimacy precisely because it's exclusive
- Diagnostic signal: if the Date Rewind feels scary, ask yourself why — discomfort with this specific person and fear of judgment are different problems with different solutions
