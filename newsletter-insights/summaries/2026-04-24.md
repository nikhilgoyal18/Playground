### In-App Surveys: The Playbook from 4M PostHog Responses `Product`
- Analysis of **4.2 million survey responses across nearly 6,000 in-app surveys** (in partnership with PostHog) — overturns most common PM assumptions about surveys
- **Exit surveys have the highest response rate: 15.5%** vs general satisfaction surveys at 8.4%; PostHog gets 42% on exit surveys for their Session Replay product — users who've decided to leave give the most specific, actionable feedback
- If you're only running one survey: make it an exit survey triggered on the cancellation or downgrade flow — highest-signal input for retention strategy
- **Event-triggered surveys get 1.6x more responses than URL-based targeting** (11.7% vs 8.8%) — Sprig rebuilt on event-driven architecture (surveys fire after user action, not on page load) and scaled to 10B+ API interactions/month
- Reed Hastings in *No Rules Rules*: Netflix's internal feedback ratio is 25% positive, 75% developmental — negative feedback is always more actionable than positive
- Specific actionable feedback examples from PostHog exit surveys: "I wish I could set it to sample only 1% of sessions", "limit total recordings per month", "need HIPAA and SOC2 confirmation" — each is a product decision you can make tomorrow vs "Great product!" from a satisfaction survey

### Roadmap Decisions Are Built From Visible Feedback, Not Complete Feedback `Product`
- Shreyas Doshi (Dec 2025 note): most people enter customer conversations seeking confirmation when they should be seeking learning — the framing shift changes what you hear
- **Feedback debt**: a bug report filed in Zendesk has signal the week it's filed; by the next planning cycle six months later, it's effectively gone — the collection infrastructure exists but the resurfacing infrastructure doesn't
- Most honest feedback comes from sales call transcripts and email threads — in those conversations customers aren't performing for a feedback form; they're telling you in their own words why they're annoyed
- Pendo studied 600+ products: **80% of features are rarely or never used**; estimated industry cost = **$29.5 billion/year** in R&D spent on things customers don't touch
- Amazon Fire Phone (2014) post-mortem: had more customer feedback infrastructure than most companies at the time, still failed — the team built around what the most vocal internal stakeholders wanted, not what phone buyers needed
- A feedback system that actually closes the loop requires four things: (1) pull from every source automatically, not just the ones someone remembered to check; (2) cluster feedback into themes without asking a human to tag every ticket; (3) let you query the full corpus in plain language; (4) close the loop back to the user who originally reported the issue
- Diagnostic exercise: trace your last three roadmap decisions back to the exact feedback that drove them — find the source document, find the original customer words; if the thread isn't there, that's where the work needs to happen
