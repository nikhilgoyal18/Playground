### My delusional dream was to win a Nobel Prize (it led me here instead) `Other`
- The reticular activating system (RAS) is the brain's reality filter — it surfaces opportunities, connections, and alignment signals, but only when it has a clear target to orient toward
- Without a directional goal, the RAS has nothing to filter for and cannot make relevant opportunities visible in your environment
- Big, "delusional" dreams function as directional vectors, not fixed coordinates — they point the brain toward a domain, not a specific destination
- The author's example chain: Nobel Prize goal → PhD in neuroscience → understanding brain function → personal healing → teaching others; none of these steps were the original plan
- When a path diverges from the original dream, that divergence is often not failure but an evolution the original goal made possible by putting you in motion
- "The dream was never meant to be the destination. The dream was just meant to be the direction."
- Setting a direction — even an imprecise or seemingly unrealistic one — activates filtering mechanisms that would remain dormant without any target at all
- Staying open to the path changing is as important as committing to the initial direction; the two operate together, not in opposition

## Nancy Chu
*1 issue this period*

### Why your stories don't sound L6 `Product`
- At a long-tenured company, colleagues fill in unstated thinking from shared context — they've watched you work through problems, so shorthand answers carry implicit depth; at a new company's interview, no one fills in anything
- In a 45-minute interview with a stranger, whatever you say is the entirety of the evidence — if your thinking isn't visible in the words you use, it isn't visible at all
- "Smart, but..." feedback after interviews almost never reflects weak underlying work; it reflects thinking that didn't surface clearly in the narrative the candidate gave
- In behavioral answers, L6 means showing *how you approach this category of problem*, not just narrating what you did in a single specific instance
- In product sense answers, L6 means identifying the actual crux of the problem rather than landing on something surface-level through mechanical frameworks
- In strategy questions, L6 means walking through 10x thinking — not 2x incremental improvements
- The goal is not to manufacture bigger stories with more impressive numbers, but to make the thinking that's already there visible within the time you have

## Aakash Gupta from Product Growth
*1 issue this period*

### Anthropic's New Automation Layer: A PM's Setup Guide `AI/ML`
- Anthropic shipped three automation tools in four weeks: **Cowork Scheduled Tasks** (local files, available since February on any paid plan), **Claude Routines** (cloud, launched April 14), and **Claude Managed Agents** (team-scale, requires engineer setup)
- Decision tree to pick the right tool: local files needed → Cowork; needs to fire while offline or from a webhook/GitHub event → Routines; needs to serve an entire team with scoped sessions and audit trails → Managed Agents
- Claude Routines run limits by plan: Pro = 5 runs/day, Max = 15, Team/Enterprise = 25; each run draws from the same usage allowance as chat and Claude Code, so heavy automation can eat into interactive budget
- Real-world scale: Rakuten deployed five specialist agents across product, sales, marketing, finance, and HR — enabling a move from quarterly to biweekly releases; Notion runs dozens of parallel sessions across team workspaces
- Routines beat Zapier-style tools on three axes: no separate SaaS bill (included in Anthropic seat), a reasoning step (reads a competitor pricing page and decides if something meaningful changed, rather than just chaining deterministic actions), and native Claude model access
- Three pre-build rules: (1) write instructions not questions — you're writing a procedure that runs without you, so be explicit at every step; (2) remove connectors you don't need — all integrations are included by default and can be used without asking during runs; (3) always hit "Run Now" before scheduling to verify behavior
- Critical architecture gotcha: each Routine run starts completely fresh with zero memory of prior runs — to track changes over time, write findings to Notion at the end of every run and read that document at the start of the next run
- Opus 4.7 (shipped two days after Routines) adds self-verification of outputs mid-run, which is particularly valuable for unattended automations that run for hours — the difference between an automation you trust and one you have to babysit
- The full 7-workflow playbook (prompts, sample outputs, engineer handoff brief, security doc, 4-week team rollout plan) is on GitHub at `github.com/aakashg/claude-routines-and-agents-pm-pack`
