## Suhas Motwani
*1 issue this period*

### Surviving AI `Product`
- The PM role is being compressed to exactly two muscles: **knowing how to ask the right question** (problem diagnosis) and **knowing what to make** (product judgment) — everything else is being absorbed by AI tools, smaller teams, and engineers who no longer need translation
- The "asking the right question" muscle: the gap between the stated problem and the actual one (e.g., "sales losing deals" → competitive intelligence problem, not an analytics problem) is where irreplaceable skill lives; that gap "almost never gets written down anywhere," so it can't be captured in AI training data
- Senior PMs build this model through years of "being burned by surface asks until your first instinct stops being trustworthy" — it can't be acquired from workshops or prompts
- "Knowing what to make" requires simultaneously holding customer problem, business model, competitive landscape, team capacity, and politics of three stakeholder groups — the correct call for a Series A startup is the wrong call for a Series D even when the surface problem looks identical
- The counterintuitive evidence: OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and Mistral — the labs building the tools that supposedly replace PMs — are running aggressive PM hiring waves despite having the best engineers in the world available
- Engineers are trained in precision (solving defined problems); PM judgment is built by "being the person who has to make the call and live with being publicly wrong for months" — these are separate cognitive habits built through different experiences
- Practical rep for the product judgment muscle: before any roadmap meeting, write down in two sentences what you'd build and why — notice how much consensus shifts your view, and flag the moments you go along when you know it's wrong
- The moment of risk: "If your career was built on the surrounding work, it has been commoditized. If it was built on the two muscles, the only thing you were ever supposed to be good at is now the only thing being measured"

## Emonthebrain
*1 issue this period*

### Imposter syndrome means you just leveled up (here's the proof) `Other`
- Core reframe: imposter syndrome is not proof you don't belong — it's proof you just leveled up; the discomfort is definitional to being in new territory, not diagnostic of being undeserving
- Video game analogy: just as you're not immediately great at a new level after beating the prior one, being unfamiliar ≠ being unqualified; you earned your way there by clearing what came before
- Three personal examples cited: a student applying to PhD programs feeling fraudulent, a friend starting at a doctor's office, Em herself pre-Jay Shetty podcast — each time discomfort preceded (and did not disprove) competence
- "With time and repetition, you get comfortable at the new level. You start to find your footing. You start to get good at it." — the antidote is exposure and reps, not more validation
- The counterintuitive prescription: once you get comfortable at a level, that's exactly when you should look for the *next* opportunity to feel imposter syndrome — it's a signal growth is happening, not a signal to stop
