## ByteByteGo
*1 issue this period*

### How Stripe Detects Fraudulent Transactions Within 100 ms `Engineering`
- Stripe's Radar ML model evaluates **over 1,000 signals per transaction** and renders a fraud verdict in **under 100 ms**, achieving **99.9% accuracy across billions of legitimate payments**
- Online payment fraud occurs in ~1 in every 1,000 transactions — this rarity makes it a hard ML problem (extreme class imbalance), requiring architectures that surface signal from noise efficiently
- The previous architecture was **Wide & Deep**: XGBoost (gradient-boosted trees, excels at memorization of seen patterns) + a DNN (excels at generalization to novel patterns). It worked but XGBoost created bottlenecks: hard to parallelize, incompatible with transfer learning and embeddings
- Dropping XGBoost outright would have caused a **1.5% drop in recall** (1.5% more fraud undetected) — an unacceptable regression, so the team had to replicate its value in a new architecture, not abandon it
- The replacement architecture, **Shield NeXt**, draws from the ResNeXt research paper ("Network-in-Neuron"): splits computation into multiple parallel branches, each a small neural network; outputs are summed. This enriches feature representation more effectively than simply making a DNN wider or deeper (which risks overfitting)
- Shield NeXt reduced model **training time by over 85%**, bringing it under 2 hours — experiments that required overnight jobs now run multiple times per day
- The architectural shift unlocks techniques previously impossible: **multi-task learning** (one model trained simultaneously on multiple related fraud objectives) and transfer learning
- Key lesson: sometimes the component actively improving accuracy must be replaced not because it's wrong but because it's blocking everything else — the right move is to replicate its value, not discard it

## PM Diego's Newsletter
*1 issue this period*

### Your Next User is an Agent `Product`
- Author's behavior shift: moved from tab-heavy human workflows (Gemini/ChatGPT/Claude for synthesis, manual context stitching) to running **multiple Claude Code agents in parallel** via Discord — researchers, assistants, developers, comms team — none requiring him to open a single tab
- The behavioral consequence: any product that **isn't agent-accessible immediately drops in priority**; alternatives with agent-first APIs get the time and money instead — this is the new buying criterion for power users
- Agents need three things human-shaped APIs don't provide: **(1) identity** — knowing which agent is acting on whose behalf with what permissions (MCP spec added agent auth in mid-2025 but most implementations still pass tokens without identifying the agent); **(2) idempotent workflows** — APIs that can tell the difference between "action ran" vs "action failed halfway through," since agents retry aggressively and double-charges/duplicate messages are a real risk; **(3) permissions at machine speed** — rate limits and audit trails built for humans break when an agent runs more calls in an hour than a human runs in a week
- Salesforce is **rebuilding its platform to be headless** to serve agents. Stripe launched **Machine Payments Protocol (MPP) on March 18, 2026** — a separate protocol because payments need machine-readable coordination MCP wasn't designed for; launched with **100+ adopters including Anthropic, OpenAI, Shopify, and Alchemy**
- **Kernel** (YC Summer 2025) raised **$22M in October 2025** for browser infrastructure purpose-built for agents: near-instant session spinup, fully isolated sessions — speed matters only when the user is an agent
- YC Spring 2025 batch was **46% agent companies**; Summer 2025 was close to half — the pipeline is locking in
- Most PMs are not thinking about agents-as-users yet; the gap between teams that do and don't is widening fast — the right PM question is now "does my product give agents identity, idempotency, and scoped permissions?"

## Product Coalition (Jay Stansell)
*1 issue this period*

### Design Thinking's Quiet Dissolution into Table Stakes `Product`
- In 2016, design thinking felt like "found religion" at Stanford d.school (Amit Badlani's words): iterating, reframing, permission to discard your first idea — it was genuinely novel for practitioners at the time
- By 2017, demand had outrun supply; Yasith Abeynayaka built self-study guides calling it "a mandatory skillset" for professionals. The d.school → IDEO → Google Ventures pipeline became canonical
- Sefi Keller identified the **bumper sticker problem** in 2019: the creators made it catchy on purpose (leaders need slogans to rally teams), but "catchy eats nuance for breakfast" — empathy was reduced to a sticky note on a diagram
- By 2023, the backlash arrived: Lee Fischman's piece stated the quiet part — "it's obvious and you already do it." His framing: design thinking **democratized design** (removed the priesthood), but a $3,000 MIT certificate was teaching what practitioners already knew
- Jackie Colburn's 2023 workshop guide acknowledged widespread session fatigue: people had "been burned by inefficient, unproductive, and boring sessions that feel like a waste of time"
- **The real tell**: Connor Joyce's 2024 piece on building AI products never mentions design thinking once — it's all LLM integration and deployment strategy. The principles are present ("just because it's powerful doesn't mean it's the answer for everything") but no longer branded
- Webflow CPO Rachel Wolan: "Human creativity remains vital in the design process, even with AI automation" — without ever naming design thinking. The instinct survived; the label didn't
- Broader pattern: **a framework succeeds when it becomes invisible** — once the best practitioners just call it "product management," the framework has won by dissolving

### Interview Performance as Direct Compensation Leverage `Product`
- Real comp data: a Senior Staff PM accepted ~**$400K year 1 TC** after additional approvals; felt they left money on the table by not getting coaching
- Contrasting case: a **Meta L6** candidate who "aced" their interview at another tier 1 company jumped from **$750K to $1.14M** — a **~$400K increase on the strength of the interview performance alone**, without negotiating further
- Core mechanism: when your interview fully represents your thinking, the offer lands at the **top of the band or bottom of the next band up** — the interview itself becomes the negotiation, removing the need to push explicitly
- Implication for senior PMs: at L6/L7 levels, the delta between a good and great interview isn't marginal — it's hundreds of thousands of dollars compounded over the full vest cycle
