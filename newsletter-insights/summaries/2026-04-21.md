### How DoorDash Launches a New Country in One Week `Engineering`
- DoorDash rebuilt their Dasher onboarding from spaghetti if/else branches (3 coexisting API versions, V3 still calling V2 handlers and writing to V2 tables) into a modular, orchestrated system — cutting new-market launch time from months to days
- Puerto Rico onboarding took ~1 week, Australia under 1 month, Canada 2 weeks, New Zealand required almost no new development — all reusing existing step modules
- Architecture has 3 layers: **Orchestrator** (routing-only, no business logic, picks which workflow handles a request), **Workflow Definitions** (ordered list of step references per market), **Step Modules** (self-contained execution units that don't know which workflow they're in)
- The key insight: the Orchestrator's value comes from how little it does — it's a traffic cop, not a brain; intelligence is distributed into the Step Modules
- Each Step Module owns its own data collection, validation, vendor calls, retries, and failure reporting — with no knowledge of adjacent steps, enabling true reuse across markets
- Legacy state management required querying multiple tables (e.g., `validation_complete = true`, `documents_uploaded = false` spread across separate DBs) to reconstruct a user's onboarding progress — the new system centralizes this
- Vendor integrations in the old system had no consistent pattern: some steps called internal services that called vendors, others called vendors directly — unpredictable to debug and test
- Analogy used: Lego bricks — standardized interfaces (studs/tubes) let you build anything; a workflow definition is the building instructions for a specific model

## Product Coalition
*1 issue this period*

### From Punch Cards to 'All My Shit's in There' `Product`
- Retention strategy evolved in 3 distinct eras: (1) "remind them you exist" — push notifications, re-engagement campaigns; (2) "make leaving expensive" — structural lock-in via data and workflows; (3) "make them not want to leave" — emotional connection and genuine value
- Derek Skaletsky's diagnosis of a churning search engine: "Search is the most perishable activity on the web. You search for something, find your answer, and… close the tab." — coined the **"All My Shit's In There" factor** as the honest description of SaaS retention
- Amazon Prime data point: 93% of members renew after year 1; 98% after year 2 — by year two, Prime becomes infrastructure, not a product competing for loyalty ("You don't cancel your water bill")
- Rob Finney (2018) on network effects via VHS vs Betamax: "A platform based network effect kicked off by a small advantage, then accentuated by a positive feedback loop" — more users = more value = less reason to leave
- Carlos Gonzalez de Villaumbrosia (2020) on PLG shift: "It's getting easier to build a business, but not easier to grow one" — when features are replicated in 6 months, the product itself must be the retention engine
- Lee Fischman on maintaining 90%+ B2B retention: "The first reason our retention was so high was simply because customers needed our stuff. When they didn't, they cancelled." — no growth hacks, just deep contextual necessity
- Asher Atlas (2025): when a music service lost its catalog overnight, reframing "broken search" as a "discovery problem" became a trust-building moment — retention lives in how you handle failures, not just successes
- Nesrine Changuel (ex-Google, Spotify, Microsoft): "User loyalty is significantly enhanced by emotional connections" — the products people stay with longest are ones they genuinely love, not ones they can't figure out how to export data from
- Key reframe: most teams measure churn reduction rather than asking *why someone would choose to stay* — wrong question leads to wrong interventions
