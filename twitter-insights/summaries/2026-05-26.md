## Summary Themes

1. **AI infrastructure is the safe bet over model labs** — Baseten's 3x ARR quarter and $11B valuation confirm investors have shifted from model-layer bets to infrastructure; Jason Lemkin's Gartner data ($435B, +60%) shows the entire category is in hypergrowth. Implication: picks-and-shovels plays (inference, retrieval, tooling) have better risk-adjusted returns than direct model competition.

2. **Google is moving AI down the UX stack** — Neural Expressive design language and AI Inbox both show Google embedding AI into the product experience layer, not just exposing it as an API. Competition is shifting from capability to design; this threatens application-layer AI products that live on top of Gmail or compete for user attention routing.

3. **Retrieval quality as the multiplier on agent capability** — Garry Tan's signal on GBrain x ActiveGraph + Marily Nika's "spawn/kill/repeat" framing together point to a maturing agentic architecture picture: fast-ephemeral sessions + high-quality retrieval = compounding agent productivity. Teams underinvesting in retrieval are building on sand.

4. **AI layoff narrative is a distraction from the real wave** — Harry Stebbings' guest claim that "none of this is AI yet" reframes current labor market stories; the actual AI displacement is 2-3 years out. Strategic planning should assume current productivity gains are pre-AI, and model a harder second wave ahead.

5. **Vertical AI following experienced domain founders** — Eric Wu (proptech) moving to construction AI follows a broader pattern of domain experts bringing AI to sectors with poor software penetration and high ROI potential (healthcare with HelloPatient, construction with NavigateAI). These vertical bets are lower competition than horizontal AI tools.
