## Summary Themes

1. **AI regulatory ratchet is tightening** — UK's Google opt-out ruling and AI water usage scrutiny both signal that AI infrastructure and content practices are entering formal regulatory scope. Builders need to design for opt-out compliance and environmental disclosure now, not as a retrofit.

2. **Distribution as the post-AI moat** — Gazdecki (from M&A transaction data) and the data quality thread from BeingPractical both point to the same thesis: as code gets cheaper to write, the durable asset is trust, relationships, and data quality. The product is commoditizing; the distribution and foundation are not.

3. **Chinese AI capital independence accelerating** — DeepSeek's self-funded $7B round (40% from CEO) and the May 26 data on China's Hugging Face download leadership together form a pattern: China's AI ecosystem is intentionally building insulation from Western capital and governance structures. Geopolitical risk analysis needs to include ownership and funding structure, not just technical capability.

4. **Gray market demand as legalization signal** — $5-10B in gray market peptide spend despite terrible UX is a playbook that applies beyond biotech: wherever regulated incumbents are slow and consumer demand is proven in underground/gray markets, the legalization opportunity is massive and near-term.

5. **Live sports as streaming infrastructure** — UFC's $7.7B Paramount deal follows NBA and NFL; live events with guaranteed audiences are now priced as platform survival assets, not entertainment deals. The streaming wars have converged on live sports as the final differentiation layer.
