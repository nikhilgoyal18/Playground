## Summary Themes

1. **Software moats are dead, and everyone is now learning it** — Evan Spiegel (via Lenny), Sheel Mohnot, and the Mistral/Europe story independently converge: AI capability parity is arriving fast, and companies betting on model superiority will repeat Snap's playbook mistake. Founders should be building on distribution, data, and brand — not features.

2. **Capital efficiency is being reset by AI-native companies** — AppLovin's $10M EBITDA-per-head and 82% margins at 450 employees is the new reference benchmark. Combined with Meta/Microsoft cutting 23K jobs for AI capex, the message is consistent: the next decade of business-building will use dramatically less headcount per dollar of output.

3. **AI data scarcity is creating new asset classes** — Defunct startup Slack archives and Jira tickets are now premium AI training data. This signals that proprietary operational data (not just structured datasets) has real market value. Teams generating rich operational history should be thinking about what they're sitting on.

4. **Institutional credibility is fracturing** — Elon's "grad school indoctrination" post at 16K likes, Garry Tan's media framing, and the Amazon price-fixing unsealing all point to the same theme: established institutions (academia, media, big platforms) are losing legitimacy signals in the tech/founder class faster than their actual behavior has changed.

5. **Geopolitical AI differentiation is a real moat** — Mistral's $14B valuation built on sovereign/non-American positioning, combined with Europe's $17B funding initiatives, confirms that "where your AI is from" is a product attribute with real purchasing weight — especially in regulated industries and non-US geographies.
