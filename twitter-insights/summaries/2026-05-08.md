### Markets Underpricing Risk: The TACO + BLISS Thesis `Business`
- Former IMF First Deputy Managing Director argues markets are ignoring risk via two mechanisms: **TACO** ("Trump always chickens out") and **BLISS** (belief in resilience underwritten by "big lasting state support")
- TACO assumes Trump reliably backs down from extreme positions; BLISS assumes governments will always provide economic backstops — both are conditional assumptions being priced as certainties
- Both create fragility: if either assumption fails in a specific instance, positions built on these theses unwind rapidly and asymmetrically
- 37 likes, 7 RTs from a credentialed macroeconomist with direct IMF-level visibility — this is not speculative commentary
- Implication: portfolio construction should stress-test TACO/BLISS failure scenarios, particularly in sectors with high policy exposure: tariffs, trade policy, defense contracts, and government-adjacent tech

### Munger Inversion: Systematize Problem-Solving by First Making It Worse `Business`
- Codie cites Charlie Munger's inversion principle: to solve any problem, enumerate all the ways to make it worse — then stop doing all of those things
- This is a systematic decision framework, not a motivational heuristic: inversion forces explicit enumeration of failure modes before selecting solutions, surfacing blind spots that forward-thinking misses
- 62 likes reflects broad resonance; the framework's simplicity makes it highly portable across contexts: product strategy, org design, pricing, hiring
- Implication: particularly powerful in strategy sessions — replacing "how do we achieve X" with "how do we guarantee we never achieve X" surfaces the real constraints and known failure modes first

1. **Agent infrastructure is becoming commercial reality** — Visa cards for AI agents (Forbes), Ardent sub-6-second DB cloning (YC), and PlayStation AI integration all signal that agentic systems are transitioning from demos to deployed infrastructure; the picks-and-shovels layer is solidifying around agents as the primary unit of compute

3. **Google's bundling strategy vs. the single-product AI app thesis** — Lenny's 150M-subscriber Google AI data and Gurley's subscription trust thesis together suggest AI product strategy is bifurcating: massive integrated bundles with switching costs (Google) vs. trusted single-purpose subscriptions; standalone AI apps competing on raw capability alone face structural disadvantage in both scenarios

4. **Policy as underweighted market signal** — Gita Gopinath's TACO/BLISS risk framework and Jack Raines' Intel 5-6x retrospective both surface the same finding: visible government commitment (stakes, legislation, rhetoric) creates real economic backstops that markets systematically underprice until the catalyst is obvious

5. **PM and knowledge worker role compression** — Harry Stebbings (Microsoft core business flat without AI), Jason Lemkin (Tragedy Apps), and Suhas Motwani's newsletter all describe the same structural dynamic at different levels: the work that surrounded the core skill is being commoditized; what remains is irreducibly human judgment that has always been the core but was previously obscured by surrounding activity
